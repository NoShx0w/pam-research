# Theory Memo 007 — PAM → RIG Compatibility Glossary v0.1

**Status:** Canonical compatibility glossary  
**Scope:** PAM observatory vocabulary through OBS-083

## Purpose

This glossary maps earlier PAM concepts into the current RIG vocabulary while preserving historical meaning, artifact scope, and overclaim guardrails.

The mapping is not an assertion that all earlier concepts are identical to RIG primitives.

## Core RIG primitives

### Relation

A structural distinction, comparison, or equivalence tested for reuse.

**PAM ancestry:** regime comparison, structural identity, transition relation.  
**Overclaim to avoid:** relation as universal semantic law.

### Carrier

A representational or measurement substrate through which a relation is expressed.

**PAM examples:** stability core, geometry fields, path shares, response fields, window features, route summaries.  
**Overclaim to avoid:** carrier as causal mechanism.

### Context

The artifact, provenance, model, corpus, and study boundary within which a claim is valid.

### Contract

A declared testing condition specifying fixed structure, allowed variation, and survival criteria.

### Transformation

The concrete operation performed under a contract.

### Survival

The degree to which a relation remains stable under the declared transformation.

### Reusable invariant

A relation that survives enough declared variation to be reused within the current artifact lineage.

### Registered invariant

A reusable-invariance candidate represented as a versioned relation × carrier record.

### Diagnostic invariant

A registered invariant with stable structural interpretation but insufficient evidence for candidate-ready repair or intervention claims.

### Negative-control contrast

Evidence that survival is specific to the target record rather than generic carrier, relation, contract, or provenance structure.

### Failure localization

The degree to which degradation can be assigned to an inspectable artifact support.

### Repair annotation

A registry note suggesting a future direction of inquiry.

### Repair specificity

The degree to which a proposed repair names the target, support, metric, control, and falsifier.

### Geometry-needed

An assessment of how much geometry is required for survival, sharpening, localization, or repair specificity.

## Registry and readiness terms

### Registry

A set of relation × carrier evidence records.

### Carrier role

The role a carrier plays for a specific relation under a specific contract: compact, primary, redundant, weak redundant, geometry-sharpening, context-sensitive, insufficient, or overbroad.

### Diagnostic-only

A record that supports interpretation but not candidate-ready actionability.

### Candidate-ready

A future maturity state requiring strong contrast, direct localization, and specific repair evidence.

### Readiness limiter

The primary evidence dimension preventing promotion.

### Failure site

An artifact support where criterion-specific degradation is directly evidenced.

### Repair target

A confirmed failure support attached to a separate falsifiable repair hypothesis.

## PAM observatory substrate terms

### Phase

An artifact-derived coordinate or regime organization field.

**RIG mapping:** relation-orienting field or regime context.  
**Avoid:** physical phase claim without qualification.

### Signed phase

A directional phase coordinate used to distinguish regimes.

### Seam

A transition-rich corridor or structural boundary object.

**RIG mapping:** candidate failure-support region only when criterion-specific degradation is directly evidenced.  
**Non-identity:** seam is not automatically a failure surface.

### Boundary

A broader transition or regime-separation region.

### Fisher geometry

The finite operational metric substrate used to compare artifact states.

### Manifold

The embedded or relational state organization induced by the current measurement procedure.

**Avoid:** treating the finite artifact embedding as an intrinsic continuum manifold without qualification.

### Geodesic, path, and route

Operational traces through the artifact geometry or transition system.

**RIG mapping:** transformation trace, carrier, or candidate failure context.

### Support

A localized artifact structure carrying energy, salience, stability, or relation evidence.

### Scale-space

The family of representations produced across declared diffusion or aggregation scales.

### Transition

An artifact-defined change between states, cohorts, regimes, or route conditions.

### Cohort

A grouped artifact population sharing a declared path, transition, recovery, or settlement property.

### Window

A bounded local interval used for transition-relative measurement.

### Coupling

A measured relation between artifact variables.

**Avoid:** coupling as control or causality.

### Boundedness

A local stability property represented in the OBS-078 compact core.

### Divergence

A measured separation or instability quantity.

### Stability

Persistence or bounded behavior under declared conditions.

### Lazarus

A multiplicative instability or recovery-pressure diagnostic used in earlier observatory layers.

**RIG mapping:** candidate carrier-local or failure-pressure signal.

### Response operator, response field, response-guided flow

Artifact-derived directional or tensorial structures used to inspect local organization and path behavior.

**Avoid:** interpreting them as causal-control operators.

### Identity and structural identity

Operational summaries of local relational organization.

**RIG mapping:** precursor to relation and structural equivalence.

### Structural equivalence

Operational sameness of local organization despite raw-coordinate differences.

**RIG mapping:** precursor to reusable relation, but not identical to registered invariance.

### Obstruction and holonomy-like residual

Operational path-dependence or transport mismatch.

**RIG mapping:** candidate precursor to failed returnability or path-dependent reuse.  
**Avoid:** formal holonomy claim.

## Historical or bracketed terms

The following remain useful but require explicit qualification:

- curvature and scalar curvature;
- identity spin;
- attractor and basin;
- proto-groupoid;
- returnability;
- partial inverse;
- gateway;
- directed escape;
- quasi-invertible core;
- transition algebra;
- topological identity;
- freeze macrostate.

These terms should not be promoted into formal mathematical claims without new evidence.

## Excluded active claims

The current PAM/RIG program does not claim:

- formal topology;
- strict groupoids;
- strict invertibility;
- causal mechanism;
- causal transfer;
- intervention or control;
- hidden-state topology;
- physical skyrmions;
- topologically protected semantic tubes;
- winding numbers;
- formal singularities;
- tokenizer-level mechanism;
- universal learning theory;
- universal attractors;
- generated-text semantic mechanisms.

## Important non-identities

- seam ≠ failure surface;
- obstruction ≠ failure localization;
- returnability ≠ repair;
- proto-groupoid ≠ formal groupoid;
- basin ≠ formal attractor;
- geometry-needed ≠ proof of intrinsic geometry;
- carrier convergence ≠ actionability;
- survival ≠ specificity;
- registration ≠ intervention-readiness;
- compactness ≠ repair-readiness;
- stability ≠ causality;
- coupling ≠ control;
- phase boundary ≠ formal topological boundary;
- holonomy-like residual ≠ formal holonomy;
- repair annotation ≠ repair hypothesis.

## Canonical summary

> PAM provides the observatory vocabulary and artifact structures; RIG registers which relations remain reusable under declared transformations and audits the maturity of those claims. Historical terms remain useful only when their operational and non-formal status is preserved.
